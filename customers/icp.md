# Ideal customer profile

Last updated: 2026-08-18

> **Status: thesis, not evidence.** No external buyer has been interviewed. Every claim below is a hypothesis with a stated test. Update as `interviews/` fills up.

---

## The buyer

**Head of Consulting / Head of Commercial** at a practice that sells complex, multi-disciplinary data & AI engagements.

They own the number. They personally build or personally sign off the model behind every significant bid. They feel the pain directly, they control budget, and they are senior enough to mandate a tool — which makes them both the user and the buyer. That alignment is unusual and valuable.

## Segments

### A — Specialist data & AI consultancies (50–500 people)
**Why they hurt:** complex delivery, mixed skills, aggressive commercial structures to compete against larger firms, and no internal tooling team to solve it.
**Why they buy:** every bid matters materially; a lost bid is a visible event.
**Why they are the beachhead:** short decision chains, one or two people can mandate adoption, and they are close to our own shape — we understand their world without research.

### B — Top-tier consulting practices (data & AI units within larger firms)
**Why they hurt:** the same problem at greater scale, plus internal governance and sign-off complexity.
**Why they buy:** margin governance and repeatability are institutional priorities.
**Why they are hard:** procurement, security review, existing enterprise PSA investments, and internal "we'll build it ourselves" instincts.
**Read:** desirable revenue, wrong first target. Land segment A, then enter B through a specific practice lead rather than centrally.

### C — Boutique / independent (5–50 people)
**Why they hurt:** the same pain, less money and less process.
**Read:** likely too small to pay meaningfully, but valuable early evidence and possible advocates. Not a target for paid adoption.

## Disqualifiers

- Firms selling standardised, repeatable work at stable rates — the commercial flex problem barely exists for them
- Staff-augmentation and body-shop models — no delivery plan to model
- Firms with a mature, adopted PSA tool *and* a bid-stage modelling practice built on it
- Anyone whose engagements are consistently small and single-discipline

## Trigger events

When the pain becomes urgent enough to act:

- A bid lost on commercials that could have been reshaped in time
- A fixed-price engagement that overran and was found to have been mispriced in the model
- A new Head of Consulting or Commercial arriving and finding no repeatable process
- Rapid team growth breaking whatever informal resourcing process existed
- A client demanding outcome-based pricing the firm cannot confidently model
- An audit or governance push on deal margins

## Their world today

- The model lives in one person's Excel file, usually built from a copy of the last engagement
- The delivery lead and the commercial lead work in separate copies and reconcile by conversation
- The resourcing team gets a screenshot, a paste, or a request in Slack
- The client gets a rebuild in slides
- The SLT gets a summary tab nobody else uses
- The commercial flex happens the night before the deadline

## Design partner criteria

For M5. Three to five practices, each of which must:

- Run at least five complex, multi-workstream engagements a year
- Have a named person who personally builds the models
- Be willing to share a real (redacted) model
- Have lost or nearly lost a bid on commercial structure in the last year
- Give us direct access to the resourcing team as well as the buyer
