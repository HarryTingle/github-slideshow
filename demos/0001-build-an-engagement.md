# 0001 — Build an engagement from scratch

Proves: M1 — the modelling engine
Specs exercised: 0001, 0002
Audience: internal
Runtime: target 10 minutes
Status: Planned

## The setup

A 12-week data platform and ML engagement for a mid-size client. Three workstreams, a team of five across four grades, one architect available only half-time, and a public holiday in the middle. The kind of engagement that takes half a day to model properly in Excel and is wrong by the end of the week.

## The tension

The plan is not the hard part. The hard part is that availability is messy, the grade mix drives the price, and the moment anything moves, every number downstream has to be rebuilt by hand — and usually is not.

## The walkthrough

| # | Action | What they see | The point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Create the engagement, set the calendar | Empty 12-week timeline | Starting from structure, not a blank grid |
| 2 | Add three phases and three workstreams | Timeline takes shape | The plan is a first-class object, not a drawing |
| 3 | Add assignments by role and grade, no named people | FTE by week and effort by grade appear immediately | A model that is fully costed before anyone is staffed |
| 4 | Set the architect to 0.4 FTE | Effort, cost and margin all move at once | One edit, every number correct |
| 5 | Add a public holiday and two weeks of leave | Effort drops in the affected weeks only | Capacity that reflects reality, not an idealised week |
| 6 | Attach two named people | Costs shift to personal rates where they exist | Role-first, people-later, without rebuilding |
| 7 | Open the trace on gross margin | The assignments and rates behind the figure | The answer to "where did this number come from?" |
| 8 | Compare against the reference Excel model | Identical totals | **The proof.** |

## The payoff

Step 8. The engine reproduces a real Excel model to the penny — so every number after this point can be trusted.
